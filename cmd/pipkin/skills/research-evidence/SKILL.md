# Research evidence

Write the research question, inclusion criteria, and date range. Prefer primary sources for material claims and capture exact source identifiers, publication dates, methodology, and access limitations. Maintain a claim-to-source map. Distinguish findings, interpretations, and open questions. Recheck calculations with units, denominators, and assumptions. Flag contradictory or missing evidence explicitly. A citation must support the precise claim. Never invent sources or imply that a source was retrieved if it was only supplied or mentioned.

## Search and evidence extraction

- Define terms, population, time window, and inclusion criteria before combining sources. Record search or selection criteria when they affect interpretation.
- For each source, capture the exact relevant result, its population or dataset, the method, and any limitations. Prefer original reports or datasets when available.
- Record competing definitions and missing data. Compare like periods, units, and populations before aggregating or ranking.

## Synthesis and review

- Connect each conclusion to the observations that support it. Use ranges, sensitivity checks, and explicit uncertainty where precision would overstate the evidence.
- Reconcile source disagreements where their scope or method explains the difference. If evidence conflicts, retain the disagreement and say what would resolve it.
- Recompute important totals, rates, and derived figures. Make the assumptions and formula reproducible from the deliverables.
- Check every citation and distinguish sources directly inspected from leads that still require verification.
